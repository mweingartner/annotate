# Annotate interface

Annotate follows Atrium, Michael's HIG-based design system (`Sources/Atrium`, vendored from `~/dev/Atrium` so this public repository builds on its own). This note records the reader's design intent and the decisions behind it.

## Window

- **One document window**: a `NavigationSplitView` with a glass sidebar, the PDF as the detail, and one `.inspector` for whatever is being worked on. SwiftUI supplies the unified glass toolbar through `NSHostingController.sceneBridgingOptions`; `NSDocument` keeps the title, proxy icon and its "Edited" subtitle.
- **Toolbar, three groups, symbols only, a tooltip on each**: the workspace modes in the centre (Read, Edit, Pages, Forms, Sign, Convert & OCR, Redact, Assistant, as toggles so each keeps its own tooltip), then zoom, Add Bookmark and a Document menu (Save, Export, Print, Fit Width, Open). The search field sits in the toolbar; typing replaces the sidebar list with results.
- **The page is paper on a desk**: PDFKit's page shadows on the system under-page colour, with Atrium margins between pages. The only controls over the page float in glass: problems at the top (they stay until dismissed) and the page indicator at the bottom, which also shows a long operation's progress and brief confirmations such as "Marker added".
- **Inspector**: the active tool, or the marker being written. Closing it finishes the tool (or discards the draft), exactly like its close button. Pane titles are New York; everything operable is SF Pro. Sections are labels and space (`PageSection`), not boxes.
- **Accent follows the user.** The old whole-window teal tint is gone. Lagoon is the app's `AccentColor` asset (compiled by `Scripts/build.sh` with `actool`), so it shows when someone chooses Multicolor, and on the welcome page.

## Markers: annotations and bookmarks

- A **bookmark** marks a page (a marker with no passage); an **annotation** marks a passage. The sidebar lists them in those two sections, in page order, as native sidebar rows: the marker's glyph, the passage (or page), the reader's note, and its categories. Clicking a row goes there; the context menu and a trailing swipe edit or delete. A filter menu narrows the list by category.
- On the page, each marker wears a **pin**: its SF Symbol in its colour, in tinted Liquid Glass with continuous corners, a sheen across the upper half, a light rim and a soft lift off the paper (`MarkerPin`). The icon fills about 70 % of the pin, so the colour sits just outside it, and the pin is centred on the line it marks. A bookmark's pin is a tab. Clicking a pin opens the marker's details in a popover; the selected marker's pin carries an accent ring. On screen a pin stays between 20 and 28 pt whatever the zoom, and its click target matches. For VoiceOver each pin is a button that opens the same details; from the sidebar, Show Details or Space does too.
- **Exception to Atrium rule 1, at Michael's request.** Pins are glass although they sit over content: they are the page's floating controls for its markers, and Michael asked for annotations and bookmarks in the OS 27 glass look. The sidebar glyphs (`MarkerGlyph`) share the pin's body, sheen and rim without the glass.
- **Printed and exported pages show the same pin** (`MarkerPinArtwork`, drawn with Core Graphics: one opaque gradient for body and sheen, because PDF output drops a gradient's transparency, and the icon as a stencil). The comment's speech bubble is not drawn in output; an export lists every note in its index.
- **Files stay portable.** Markers are still saved as standard annotations (highlight, a small icon box and a comment) so any PDF reader shows them. In Annotate's viewer the icon box and comment of each marker the reader actually read are `ViewerMarkerAnnotation`s that skip drawing (an annotation that merely claims Annotate's owner key draws as saved), so they don't double up under the pins. PDFView draws highlights itself without asking the annotation, so a bookmark's small highlight is covered by its tab instead. Printing, flattened export, conversion, OCR and redaction run inside `MarkerChrome.drawingForOutput` (and printing is recognised by `NSPrintOperation.current`), so output always draws every marker annotation exactly as saved.

## Editing text in place

- In Edit, **click text to edit its paragraph** where it is, with the insertion point where you clicked. Dragging across words edits just those words. Escape, Done, or a click elsewhere ends the edit.
- A paragraph is recovered from line geometry (`ParagraphText`): consecutive lines of about the same height, about one line apart, sharing a left edge (a first-line indent is allowed). Its line ends become spaces so edits rewrap within the paragraph (a word hyphenated across lines joins without a space, and a line starting a list item keeps its break), and the original distance between lines is kept as line spacing.
- **Set exactly like the original.** The engine reads each original glyph's pen position (`PDFNativeTextLayout`), and the edit is set from it (`MatchedLayout`): the first baseline lands exactly on the original's, lines keep the original pitch (a fixed line height, calibrated against what CoreText actually adds), and the block spans the original margins. Alignment is read from the lines: justified (shared edges, shorter last line; justified ends may overhang by a tenth of the font size), right-aligned or centred (edges within half a point), otherwise left, with any first-line indent. Ragged text is set to the original column, recovered from where its lines broke, so unchanged lines break where they did. Character spacing is kept. Measured on ragged, justified, right-aligned, centred and indented paragraphs: every baseline and edge within 0.05 pt.
- **The original font, or the closest one.** An installed source font is used as is. A missing one is replaced by the installed font of the same kind (serif, sans, fixed pitch), weight and slant whose glyph widths are nearest the original's, with separate letter and word-space tracking that brings its widths to the original's (letters up to 10 %, spaces up to a third). A PDF's own embedded font is never used for new text: a document's font must not draw what the person types, and macOS subsets can't map new characters anyway. The inspector names the substitute. New text boxes take the font and colour of the nearest text on the page, and name any substitute too. A substitute can still move a line break by a word; installed fonts break exactly.
- **Layouts are checked before use.** A layout is used only when it reads down the page in order and is believable (character spacing at most half the font size, line pitch between half and five times it, indent within the block), and only when it places the block on the page. Lines that line up only on a right edge or centre (a title block, a signature) keep their line breaks. The recovered column reaches past the longest line only where the page beside the paragraph is empty.
- **The page shows the real text.** Every keystroke is applied to the PDF itself, so the page already shows the edited words in their true fonts and positions. The editor on top (`LiveTextCanvas`) draws only the insertion point, a translucent selection and a hairline outline; its own glyphs stay hidden. If the latest text cannot be applied, the editor shows it on a page-coloured ground with a dashed caution outline, so nothing typed is ever invisible.
- **No flashing.** Typing replaces only the edited page inside the open document, which the reader redraws immediately; replacing the whole document blanked the page for about 150 ms per keystroke. Outline entries and links that pointed at the old page are pointed at the new one. Every link and outline destination (into the page, and out of it) is resolved before the swap and rebuilt after it, so navigation survives saving. A page with form fields, or a document with an outline of more than 10,000 entries, keeps the whole-document path.
- **Room to grow.** Text that no longer fits grows its block downward, like a text box in Pages, but only over empty page: never over other text and never off the page. Otherwise the block clips like a Pages text box, with an overflow mark on its bottom edge, and the inspector offers Fit Height to Text or Discard Changes; no banner competes with it. Editing text on a page clears that page's queued redaction areas, because moved text may no longer lie under them.
- A small **glass format bar** floats beside the block (font family, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, Done). The inspector keeps every control: typeface, alignment, spacing, position and size, and the text as a list for VoiceOver or larger type.

## Verifying

`ParagraphEditingTests` and `MarkerChromeTests` cover paragraph detection, reflow and line pitch, the click-to-edit path, growth only into empty space, viewer-only drawing and output drawing. Debug builds accept `ANNOTATE_APPEARANCE=light|dark` for screenshots in either appearance without changing the system setting.
